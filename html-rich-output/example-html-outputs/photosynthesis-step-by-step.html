Photosynthesis, step by step

A visual walkthrough of how plants turn sunlight, carbon dioxide, and water into sugar, oxygen, and stored chemical energy.

TL;DR — Photosynthesis happens in chloroplasts. Light reactions capture sunlight to split water and make energy carriers, then the Calvin cycle uses those carriers to build sugar from carbon dioxide. Oxygen is released as a byproduct, while glucose stores energy the plant can use later.

Inputs
Light, CO₂, H₂O
Location
Chloroplasts
Two phases
Light reactions + Calvin cycle
Outputs
Sugar + O₂
Steps
01 Capture light 02 Split water 03 Make ATP/NADPH 04 Fix carbon 05 Build sugar
01

Chlorophyll captures light

Sunlight hits chlorophyll pigments inside the thylakoid membranes of chloroplasts. The light energy excites electrons, starting an electron flow.

02

Water is split, oxygen leaves

The plant replaces lost electrons by splitting water molecules. This releases electrons, hydrogen ions, and oxygen gas.

Reaction fragment

2H₂O → O₂ + 4H⁺ + 4e⁻

OUTPUT

The oxygen animals breathe is mostly this leftover product from water splitting.

03

Electron flow makes ATP and NADPH

Excited electrons move through protein complexes in the thylakoid membrane. Their energy pumps hydrogen ions, creating a gradient that powers ATP synthase.

04

Carbon dioxide is fixed

In the Calvin cycle, the enzyme Rubisco attaches CO₂ to a five-carbon molecule called RuBP. The unstable product quickly becomes two three-carbon molecules.

Why “fixed” means usable

Carbon fixation means inorganic carbon from air becomes part of an organic molecule. That makes the carbon usable for building sugars and plant tissues.

05

The Calvin cycle builds sugar

ATP supplies energy and NADPH supplies high-energy electrons. After several turns, the cycle exports G3P, a small sugar that can be assembled into glucose, starch, cellulose, and other plant molecules.

Whole-process equation

This summary equation hides many enzyme-driven steps, but it captures the overall material flow.

6CO₂ + 6H₂O + light → C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂

CARBON

CO₂ provides carbon atoms for sugar.

WATER

H₂O supplies electrons and releases O₂.

LIGHT

Light becomes stored chemical energy.
